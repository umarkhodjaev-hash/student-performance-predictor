BEHIND THE MODEL

How does the predictor work?

Student Performance Predictor uses machine learning to analyze academic habits and previous performance, then estimates a student's exam score on a 0–100 scale.

01

Student Input

You enter information such as study time, attendance, sleep, previous scores, motivation and other academic habits.

Machine Learning

04

Performance Report

The model estimates your exam score and the website presents insights and personalized recommendations.

What information does the model use?

The model currently uses eight academic and behavioral features.

01 Hours Studied

Weekly study time.

02 Attendance

Percentage of attended classes.

03 Sleep Hours

Average sleep duration.

04 Previous Scores

Previous academic performance.

05 Motivation Level

Self-reported academic motivation.

06 Tutoring Sessions

Academic support sessions.

07 Physical Activity

Regular physical activity.

08 Extracurricular Activities

Participation outside regular classes.

How was the model trained?

STEP 1 Dataset

Student performance records were loaded and the eight selected variables were separated from the final exam score.

STEP 2 Train / Test Split

80% of the records were used to train the model and 20% were kept unseen for evaluation.

STEP 3 Preprocessing

Missing values are handled automatically and categorical variables are converted into machine-readable features.

STEP 4 Evaluation

Predictions on unseen test data were evaluated using Mean Absolute Error and the R² score.

What do the metrics mean?

MAE

1.38

Mean Absolute Error measures the average difference between the model's predicted exam score and the actual score.

R²

0.606

R² describes how much of the variation in exam scores is explained by the information provided to the model.

IMPORTANT

Prediction is not certainty.

The predicted score is an estimate based on patterns in the training data. Academic performance can also depend on factors that are not included in this model, such as teaching quality, stress, subject difficulty, learning environment and individual circumstances.

The recommendations shown after a prediction are rule-based guidance generated from the information entered by the user. They should not be interpreted as proof that changing one habit will directly cause a specific improvement in exam performance.

Dataset Source

This project uses the Student Performance Factors dataset, which contains 6,607 student records and information related to study habits, attendance, sleep, motivation, tutoring, physical activity and exam performance.

The dataset was used to train and evaluate the machine-learning model that powers this predictor.
